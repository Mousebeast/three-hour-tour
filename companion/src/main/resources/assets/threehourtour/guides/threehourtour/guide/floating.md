# Staying afloat

Your ship is a real vessel with real mass. Everything you put on it weighs
something, and weight against volume is what decides whether it floats and how
deep it sits.

Here is the problem you will hit the first time you try to build anything
bigger than the raft: **ordinary wood is about twice the density of water.** A
flat deck of oak planks cannot float. Not "floats badly" — cannot. Oak is
where ships go to sink.

## Treated wood is the answer

<ItemLink id="threehourtour:treated_planks" /> is the structural material for
anything that has to stay on top of the sea. It is light enough to float a
deck, and the chain that makes it is what a bigger ship actually costs.

<ItemLink id="threehourtour:treated_slab" /> and
<ItemLink id="threehourtour:treated_stairs" /> weigh **half** what the full
block does, because they occupy half the space. A deck built of slabs is a
real and legitimate way to buy yourself displacement.

## You cannot power your way out of it

**Buoyancy cannot be compensated for.** No amount of engine fixes a hull that
sits too low. A boat riding low takes water over the rim and goes down, and
thrust does nothing about it except get you there faster.

Displacement, not horsepower, is the limit on how big your ship gets.
