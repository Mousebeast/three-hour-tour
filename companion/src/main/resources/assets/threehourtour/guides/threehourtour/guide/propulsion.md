# Moving

Thrust comes from a propeller turned by a power source. One thing to understand
before you start bolting engines to things:

> **The propeller caps your speed, not the engine.**

More power buys you *acceleration* — how fast you get up to speed, and how well
you hold it against a heavy hull. It never buys a higher top speed. Strapping a
second engine to a wooden propeller gets you to the same speed, sooner.

## The ladder

| | What it is | When you have it |
|---|---|---|
| <ItemLink id="create:hand_crank" /> | rowing, essentially. Miserable | always |
| <ItemLink id="aeronautics:wooden_propeller" /> | improvised, from what the sea gives | always |
| <ItemLink id="simulated:white_portable_engine" /> | burns solid fuel for rotation. Comes in sixteen colours, all identical | on the starting raft |
| <ItemLink id="aeronautics:andesite_propeller" /> | the real one | once you have andesite |
| <ItemLink id="createthrusters:thruster" /> | the proper propulsion line | mid-tree |

The hand crank and the wooden propeller are craftable from the first minute and
they never stop being craftable. If your engine goes over the side in deep
water, you are not stranded — you are rowing. Slowly. Thinking about what you
did.

The crank is three planks and an iron nugget; the propeller is four wooden slabs
around a stick, which is the same shape as the real one, in wood. Everything in
both comes off your own deck, the bonsai pot, or the scoop.

You cannot work the improvised propeller back up into an andesite one. That
route is deliberately closed — the andesite propeller is earned through a press
and an alloy, not by starting from slabs.

## Fuel, and why the pot comes first

The portable engine burns **anything a furnace burns** — it reads ordinary fuel
burn times, so logs, planks, sticks, coal and charcoal all work.

It arrives on the raft **empty**, and that matters more than it sounds. Your
scoop only yields while you are under way, so until something goes into the
engine, nothing comes out of the sea: no kelp, no clay, no string, and therefore
no research. Coal is in none of the three mesh tables, so it will not solve this
for you.

Day one you have two fuels, and both are already aboard:

- **Deck planks.** An axe on the raft gives you planks, and planks burn. It
  works, it is instant, and you are feeding your own ship into the fire.
- **Bonsai logs.** The <ItemLink id="bonsaitrees4:bonsaipot" /> in the chest
  takes the block of dirt, the oak sapling and the wooden axe, and cuts a tree
  every ten seconds for as long as you leave it alone.

The pot is the one that does not run out. It is also **the only thing on the
raft that works while you are stationary**, which makes it — not the engine, and
not the scoop — the first thing to set up.

Charcoal comes later and is strictly better: logs and a furnace, and the furnace
wants stone, which is what the first research node gives you.

## Steering

A <ItemLink id="create:copper_valve_handle" /> on a bearing. It looks like a
ship's wheel and needs nothing you cannot have on day one.

It improves twice: a <ItemLink id="createthrusters:analogue_contraption_controller" />
early in the tree, and a proper helm with a wheel and a throttle later on.
