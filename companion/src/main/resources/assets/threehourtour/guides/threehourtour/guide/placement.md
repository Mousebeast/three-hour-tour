# What goes where

Some blocks check whether they are on a ship before they will place, and they
refuse out loud rather than failing silently. If something will not go down,
read the message — it is telling you the actual rule.

- **Some blocks may only be placed aboard.** The things that make your ship a
  ship: your **sea scoop**, and the growing pots — **botany pots** and **bonsai
  pots**. None of these will go down on land, and none of them will go down on
  a raft you have not assembled yet.
- **Some may never be placed aboard.** Usually because they would misbehave on
  a moving ship.
- **The ship core cannot be moved by machinery.** No piston, no contraption and
  no other player can shift it. You can — see *Your ship*.

**The raft is not a vessel until you pull the lever.** Everything in that first
list refuses to place until it is. If you are laying out a raft before
assembling it, build the hull first and fit the scoop and the pots afterwards.

## Outposts are fine

You can build on land. A platform over an ore vein, a shelter on an island, a
dock — whatever you like.

What you cannot do is move in. Research is spent at a terminal, the terminal
has to be aboard, and you respawn on your ship. Everything that makes somewhere
a base points back at the deck.

## Dying

**You respawn on your ship**, not at a bed ashore. A bed on land will not take
your spawn point away from the core, so there is no way to strand your respawn
on an island you sailed away from three hours ago.

Death costs you what death normally costs you — your carried items, where you
dropped them. It never costs you your ship. The core does not care that you
died.

## The one that catches people

**Swapping a scoop mesh is unconditional.** Hand the scoop a worse mesh and it
takes it, silently, and downgrades. Nothing warns you, nothing stops you, and
nothing gives you the good one back afterwards. Check what you are holding.
