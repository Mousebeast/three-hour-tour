# Drinking water

You are floating on an ocean and none of it is safe to drink. Water, water,
every where — and you have to boil it.

## Seawater will make you ill

Water taken from the open ocean is **dirty**. Drinking it is not a gamble on
whether something bad happens — it is a guaranteed dose of nausea, hunger and
the Thirsty debuff, plus a one-in-three roll for poison on top.

Water gets cleaner with depth and dirtier with movement, so a bottle from the
surface is the worst case, and the surface is where you are.

**Looking up in the rain does nothing.** In a lot of packs you can drink by
staring at the sky during a storm. Here you will just get wet.

## The day-one answer is the campfire

Your raft has one, and this is its third job.

Cook a bottle of water on the campfire and it climbs **one purity step every
fifteen seconds**. Three passes takes seawater all the way to purified. That is
the whole answer for your first hours, and it costs you nothing but fuel and
patience.

## Food does not fix thirst

**Nothing refills your thirst except a drink.** No stew, no meal, no soup — if
you are thirsty, you need water, and there is no meal in the game that is
secretly a substitute for one.

What a cooked meal does do is **stop the clock**. The Nourishment effect from a
proper dish pauses your thirst draining while it lasts. That is worth having and
it is not the same thing: eat well before a long dive or a landfall and you buy
yourself time, not water.

## Doing it in bulk needs an island

The volume upgrade is the thirst mod's **filter frames** and **water boiler**,
which turn purification into something a machine does while you are elsewhere.

Their filter cores need string, paper and **wool**. Wool means sheep, and sheep
mean an island — so clean water in quantity is a landfall reward, in exactly the
same place bricks are. Your first trip ashore pays for a lot at once.
